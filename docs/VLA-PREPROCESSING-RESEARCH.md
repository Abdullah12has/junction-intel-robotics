# VLA preprocessing research for the blue-plate station

Research date: 15 November 2026. Primary-source review supporting the separate dataset inspection and experiment plan. No training or hardware actions were performed for this review. Dataset context supplied by the parent task: SO-101, 64 episodes, three RGB camera views, 24,444 frames at 30 FPS; user says the destination is the right blue plate, while the stored instruction incorrectly names the left green plate. Do not interpret this note as an independent visual audit of all episodes.

## Decision

Fix dataset semantics, video/action integrity and train/test separation first. Establish a clean raw-image SmolVLA baseline. The parent audit reports distinct block colours, so test target-specific colour language before investing in a marker tracker. Test marker conditioning as the following experiment, using a thin marker in the overhead view and an explicit online target-selection contract. An X is a hypothesis about target ambiguity, not an established cure for this station. If failures are contact alignment, occlusion, stale images or servo tracking, a target marker alone will not solve them.

A low-cost text-target condition is worth comparing when block identities are unambiguous: “Pick up the red cube and put it on the blue plate.” Colour-only instructions cannot disambiguate identical blocks. A marker can specify an individual instance. Both require the selected target to be kept stable across a pick; neither establishes grasp success.

## Connection to the parent dataset audit

The parent inspection reports 64 single-pick episodes totalling 814.8 seconds (median 12.13 seconds), six distinctly coloured blocks, and groups of consecutive picks with accumulating plate contents. Split by scene reset/layout group, not randomly by episode. The parent reports that the overhead view contains the full workspace but small targets, the wrist view provides useful contact detail, and the fixed-close view often loses the target while showing human background. These observations favour a target-colour language experiment and an overhead+wrist camera ablation before elaborate prompting. The parent observed an inconsistency during thumbnail review, regenerated all sheets through sequential decoding, and then compared random seeking against sequential frames at nine positions with exact pixel agreement. No decoder defect was established; initial apparent empty transports were not confirmed and must not be treated as dataset failures. No target-switching failure has yet been established. The blue plate appears on image-left in overhead but is on the operator’s right, reinforcing the recommendation to refer to its colour. These are parent-audit findings, not independent observations by this research agent.

## What the primary sources establish

| Source | Evidence | Meaning for this station |
|---|---|---|
| [SmolVLA paper and author overview](https://huggingface.co/blog/smolvla) | SmolVLA uses a flow-matching action expert conditioned by visual, language and state inputs. | Multiple valid actions are not automatically a contradiction; this is a generative policy, not simply a mean-action regressor. |
| [Diffusion Policy](https://diffusion-policy.cs.columbia.edu/) | Its experiments explicitly show multimodal behavior and commitment to one mode; it predicts receding-horizon action sequences. | “Many blocks inevitably confuse a policy” is too strong. Measure actual switching versus failed execution after a stable choice. Results for this model are not a guarantee for SmolVLA. |
| [VIMA, ICML 2023](https://vimalabs.github.io/) | Multimodal prompts and object-level representations support manipulation in its procedurally generated simulation benchmark. | Instance-level goal specification has precedent. VIMA is a different architecture and benchmark, not a drop-in image transformation for an existing checkpoint. |
| [CrayonRobo, CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Li_Object-Centric_Prompt-Driven_Vision-Language-Action_Model_for_Robotic_Manipulation_CVPR_2025_paper.html) | Uses visual-language prompts for contact poses and motion directions, with a training strategy tailored to interpreting them. | Strong rationale for explicit visual goals, but its contact/pose prediction and execution differ from stock joint-action SmolVLA. It does not prove that adding a literal X to 64 demonstrations is optimal. |
| [RT-Sketch](https://rt-sketch.github.io/) | Trains goal-conditioned policies using sketches derived from hindsight goal images. | Goal images/sketches are another way to reduce task ambiguity; desired-goal information must also be supplied at deployment. A final frame is not a normal current observation. |
| [RT-Trajectory, ICLR 2024](https://rt-trajectory.github.io/) | Derives training trajectory sketches from recorded robot motion and uses human drawings or generated plans at inference. Its 2.5D variant supplies height information absent from a 2D trace. | A target point does not specify approach height, grasp orientation or route. Full future paths are a much bigger change than target markers and require a usable inference-time planner. |
| [TraceVLA](https://tracevla.github.io/) | Uses tracked **historical** image motion, overlaying traces and also providing the raw image. Its adaptation used 150K trajectories. | History traces may address motion/phase ambiguity. This differs from future trajectory sketches and is not justified as the first change for a 64-episode dataset. |
| [Exploiting Policy Idling for Dexterous Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15669) | Reports that filtering small actions or changing control frequency can harm precision/reactivity, and investigates improvement around idling states. | Do not delete every low-motion frame. Preserve settling, finger closure, precise contact adjustment and valid recovery. The paper's perturbation intervention is not a recommendation to perturb this physical station automatically. |
| [LeRobot human-in-the-loop collection](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/main/hil_data_collection) | Supports collecting human corrections at states reached by the policy and retraining on demonstrations plus corrections. | After the baseline, target observed missed approaches, drops and stalled phases with human correction data rather than gathering only more identical easy demonstrations. Verify compatibility with the installed station version before using current CLI examples. |
| [LeRobot RTC](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/main/rtc) and [Physical Intelligence RTC](https://www.pi.website/research/real_time_chunking) | Asynchronous chunk generation with overlap conditioning addresses inference delays and inconsistent chunk transitions. | Compare shorter execution horizons using measured latency. RTC is a runtime change, distinct from data preprocessing. Plain shortening adds holds under synchronous execution and is not equivalent to RTC. |
| [LeRobot dataset guidance](https://huggingface.co/blog/lerobot-datasets) | Recommends stable camera capture, clear feature naming, consistent state/action dimensions, approximately 30 FPS and adequate image quality. | These are prerequisites. An extra camera with frozen frames or a wrong semantic mapping can be worse than a well-audited smaller input set. |

## Stock SmolVLA constraints verified in upstream code

The current upstream [configuration](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/main/src/lerobot/policies/smolvla/configuration_smolvla.py), inspected on the research date, defaults to one current observation, 50-action chunks, 50 executed actions, 512×512 resize-with-padding, mean/std state and action normalization, and expert-only training with the vision encoder frozen. `observation_delta_indices` returns `[0]`. Its `use_delta_joint_actions_aloha` option explicitly raises `NotImplementedError`; it is not a generic switch for SO-101 relative actions. The installed station version may differ and must be pinned before reproducing experiments.

The [processor](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/main/src/lerobot/policies/smolvla/processor_smolvla.py) maps features, tokenizes tasks, normalizes inputs/actions and unnormalizes predicted actions. Therefore any new crop, marker, action coordinate convention or statistics must be deployed consistently with its trained checkpoint. A marker can be implemented as deterministic RGB preprocessing without adding an architectural input, but whether the frozen visual features make it usable is an empirical question. Additional cropped views or history images change the input configuration and require explicit retraining/mapping.

## Proposed preprocessing contract

These are engineering recommendations inferred from the evidence, not claims that published studies tested this exact recipe.

1. **Immutable originals and provenance.** Keep source videos/parquet checksums. Make a versioned derived dataset with a manifest mapping every retained episode/frame and change to source. Correct the task text to “Pick up a cube and put it on the blue plate”; colour avoids image-left/operator-right ambiguity. Apply the correction in both task tables and episode metadata references.
2. **Audit before filtering.** Validate row continuity, per-episode timestamps, action dimensions/order/units, finite values, camera file and timestamp intervals, actual decoded video lengths and frame freshness. Nominal 30 FPS does not prove acquisition synchronization. Identify repeated/frozen image spans separately from legitimate stationary scenes. A synchronization correction requires measured offset evidence, not an arbitrary shift that lowers validation loss.
3. **Outcome and phase annotations.** Annotate approach, close, lift, transport, release and retreat; label success, incomplete, dropped, recovery, ambiguous target and camera defect. Keep confident successful recovery segments. Quarantine incorrect actions or ambiguous/faulty intervals from the initial success-imitation baseline, preserving them for diagnostics. Split multi-pick recordings only at natural boundaries with the full approach and release available.
4. **Conservative timing edits.** Trim only obvious recording lead-in and post-completion dead time initially. Never join temporally separated actions and pretend they are adjacent 30 Hz samples. Internal cuts need separate segments/episodes and correct video offsets. Do not smooth gripper opening/closing transitions or average away distinct grasp paths. Keep original capture rate initially.
5. **Camera preparation.** Use deterministic orientation and aspect-ratio-preserving preprocessing. If fixed borders/background dominate, test one static workspace crop that retains every valid source block, destination and gripper path. Apply identically at inference. Do not move cameras to match a crop experiment. Review the actual post-resize tensors, because small blocks/markers may vanish there.
6. **Augmentation.** Start mild, task-preserving exposure/brightness changes only after an unaugmented baseline. Avoid strong hue jitter for colour-defined goals, flips that change geometry, crops that remove the target, background synthesis that changes shadows/contact, or action noise without physically valid corresponding observations.
7. **Splits and statistics.** Group by source episode and layout/recording batch; descendants of one episode stay in one split. Fit normalization on training data only, preserve joint units and gripper convention, and retain frozen held-out scenes. Grouped splits may leave fewer genuinely independent scenes than the episode count implies.

## Target-marker experiment: how to avoid an oracle-only result

During offline annotation, it is valid to inspect later frames to identify **which object was intended**, then annotate that identity before the approach. That annotation is a goal supplied to the policy. It becomes a misleading evaluation if a deployed policy would not have an equivalent selector, or if future gripper positions/trajectory/phase are quietly encoded in the input. Hindsight supervision and online observability must be separated.

Start with an operator click on the overhead frame. This removes automatic detection quality from the first test. Render a thin, consistent outline/cross around the selected object, preserve its visible shape, and use the explicit instruction “Pick up the marked cube and put it on the blue plate.” Use the same marker geometry after resize for train and inference. Keep the other views raw.

Maintain object identity for the entire attempt; do not choose the nearest block on every frame. Define the same behavior during occlusion, lift and placement in demonstrations and deployment. If a tracker cannot see the object, do not use future frames to generate an impossibly perfect live position in the test. Start by ending the marked single-pick skill only after placement/retreat; select the next object separately. Actual target tracking, occlusion handling and completion detection remain system components to validate.

Evaluate three levels independently:

- **Human-selected target:** tests whether marker conditioning helps control at all.
- **Automatically selected/tracked target:** tests the full pipeline, including perception failures.
- **Counterfactual selection:** same initial scene, marker on A versus B. If the same object is always chosen, the policy may be ignoring the prompt and replaying scene/layout biases.

Do not use randomly wrong markers paired with unchanged expert actions as augmentation. They introduce contradictory supervision. If marker jitter/dropout is later needed, measure real deployment uncertainty and test it separately.

## Prioritized experiments and acceptance criteria

| Order | Experiment | Question answered |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Audit, correct instruction, annotate outcomes, grouped split | Is the learning target trustworthy? |
| 1 | Raw-view SmolVLA baseline; separately ACT on the same clean split | Does task-specific imitation already work without prompt engineering? ACT is a non-language baseline. |
| 2 | Same SmolVLA with specific target colour text, where identity is unique | Is a simpler language annotation sufficient? |
| 3 | Same SmolVLA with marked overhead target | Does instance-level visual selection outperform text conditioning? |
| 4 | Camera ablation: overhead+wrist versus all three; or static crop if image audit supports it | Is the remaining error due to visibility/resolution or noisy/redundant input? Change only one factor at a time. |
| 5 | Execute 10 versus 25 versus 50 actions; then RTC if delay dominates | Is feedback latency/chunk consistency limiting performance? |
| 6 | Add targeted human corrections; optional broader model comparison | Does exposure to actual failure states resolve the residual problem? |

Freeze layouts and prompts across models and randomize trial order. Use several training seeds for promising conditions when practical. Start with 20–30 paired physical trials per condition as a screening experiment, then increase trials for the best variants; those small samples have broad uncertainty. Record target adherence, switches before contact, lift success, placement success, drop/intervention rates, cycle time and repeated-action/clipping statistics. Evaluate both single picks and complete multi-block scenes. Report actual successes/trials with confidence intervals rather than treating lower validation action loss as proof of better robot performance.

Do not prioritize segmentation-only images, dynamically zoomed object crops, future-path overlays, depth/point-cloud architectures, synthetic action augmentation, end-to-end RL or a larger VLA until the baseline identifies their corresponding failure mode. Dynamic crops lose global position unless that information is retained; an X contains no height or collision information; a new model cannot repair incorrect camera/action correspondence. These are experiment-scope recommendations, not blanket claims that those techniques cannot help.
