# MuJoCo synthetic data for the SO-101 blue-plate task

Primary-source review, 15 November 2026. No hardware or training was run. Recommendation: treat simulation as a measured augmentation experiment, not a replacement for the new real demonstrations. Build a real-only baseline first and compare matched real+sim sampling. Keep simulation success separate from physical success.

## Evidence that simulation can help—and hurt

**RoboCasa365 provides real-world evidence, with important boundaries.** Its GR00T N1.5/Panda experiments used 140 real demonstrations across four kitchen tasks. Sim-and-real training reached 79.8% mean success versus 61.8% real-only. The method combined mid-training on 150 simulated tasks with matched sim+real fine-tuning and re-rendered simulated camera views to resemble the real rig. This is evidence for a particular multi-stage recipe, not a result for SO-101/SmolVLA or arbitrary MuJoCo rollouts. In a separate downstream **simulation** study, adding MimicGen data to human-collected simulated demonstrations reduced downstream performance relative to human-only simulation data; the authors attribute this to variable synthetic trajectory quality. These two results must not be conflated. [RoboCasa365, §§4.4–4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04356v1)

**Task and camera alignment matter, and there is no universal mixing ratio.** Sim-and-Real Co-Training studies Panda and humanoid tasks. Removing approximate camera alignment reduced success from 67% to 56% and from 95% to 70% in two ablations. On one task, 99% simulation sampling worked best, but 99.5%/99.9% reduced success from 95% to 60%. A mismatched single-arm simulation prior at 99% sampling produced near-zero success on a bimanual task. One dynamics-alignment ablation did not improve performance; that narrow result does not establish that SO-101 joint mapping, friction or servo behavior can be ignored. [Sim-and-Real Co-Training, §V-D and Appendix VIII-M](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24361v1)

**MimicGen multiplies demonstrations; it does not guarantee transferable ones.** It transforms object-relative subtask trajectories and replays them in new scenes. The original study assumes object poses, a known subtask sequence and one reference object per segment. Its limitations explicitly include success-only filtering, biased coverage, unnatural interpolation and possible collisions. Its real-robot demonstration-generation experiments collected on real hardware; the paper left training in simulation then transferring to reality as future work. Consequently, cite it as a data-generation method, not as proof of solved sim-to-real transfer. [MimicGen, Appendices D, H and R](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.17596v1)

## Direct SO-101 evidence is weaker

- A released [SO-101 SimStudio SmolVLA model](https://huggingface.co/alexhegit/so101-simstudio-lab01-pnp-smolvla) explicitly describes MuJoCo **sim2sim** training/evaluation. Its existence demonstrates a working training pipeline, not measured improvement on a real arm.
- The author-owned [SO101 real2sim repository](https://github.com/Yangjingyuan-HK/so101-real2sim) reports difficulty replaying a physical carton grasp reliably in MuJoCo. This is a useful contact-model warning from one implementation, not evidence that MuJoCo generally cannot model SO-101 grasping. Real-to-sim replay also differs from sim-to-real learned-policy transfer.
- An [SO-101 Isaac Sim dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/alfinwilliam30/training_vla_arm) documents distinct action-unit mappings and known gaps. It contains simulator-only data, so it is not physical evaluation evidence and its joint conversion cannot be copied to this station.

The sources reviewed do not establish a reliable numerical SO-101/SmolVLA real-world improvement from the specific synthetic data available in this workspace. That is an evidence limit, not proof that it cannot help.

## Existing station evidence

The [simulation training record](../simulation/TRAINING-STATUS.md) documents 150 accepted scripted demonstrations from 562 preflight attempts, two fixed camera views, and a simulation-only SmolVLA pilot. Six held-out learned-policy trials yielded one verified grasp and zero stable placements, despite low offline loss. This demonstrates why neither expert replay success nor loss is sufficient acceptance evidence. The dataset has estimated scene/contact parameters and a nominal normalization mapping; it does not establish calibrated transfer.

[Co-training run 01](VERDA-TRAINING.md) combined 150 simulated episodes with six older real episodes, repeating real data three times (reported real frame fraction 0.39). That is a historical pilot, not an evaluation of the newly collected 64 demonstrations or evidence that the mixture beats real-only. Later horizon experiments exist separately; do not present the historical “not yet evaluated” note as the entire current state.

The [wrist-camera handoff](../simulation/WRIST-CAMERA-HANDOFF.md) is a decisive compatibility limit: the existing sim camera named `wrist` is actually **fixed-close**. The separately added `wrist_mounted` has provisional geometry and was not wired into those saved training datasets. Do not relabel fixed-close images as moving wrist images or pretend an empty input is an equivalent measured wrist view.

## Proposed experiment for this station

These choices are proposals, not paper-established optimal settings.

1. **Match the training contract.** Use the same blue-plate task, real joint/action conventions, gripper convention, control cadence and camera roles. Preserve exact conversion provenance; validate commanded targets versus measured observations. Do not compensate for mapping errors by clipping large commands.
2. **Generate coverage we lack.** Vary reachable block locations, orientations, distractor layouts, plate occupancy and chosen target. Use same-scene alternatives where different target colours are selected to test instruction dependence. Keep the blue destination semantically stable. Start with narrow measured camera/lighting variation, not arbitrary randomization.
3. **Quality-gate generated episodes.** Require actual bilateral contact/lift, transport, release and stable placement; exclude teleport/attach shortcuts, large penetration, collisions, excessive clipping, implausible joint speeds and unnatural connection trajectories. Log attempted as well as accepted scene distributions so successful-only filtering cannot hide coverage holes.
4. **Resolve camera compatibility before mixing.** Either generate and validate mounted-wrist observations for the new sim set, or run a separate comparison using only genuinely common camera roles. The existing old two-view dataset is not ready-made overhead+wrist augmentation.
5. **Keep provenance and explicit sampling.** Record real/sim source domain for every sample. A proposed 80% real / 20% simulated mix means the probability of drawing training samples, not the fraction of episode directories. Match optimization budgets and report actual sample exposure. Test normalization strategy consistently across conditions; do not let a large sim archive silently dominate statistics or minibatches.
6. **Evaluate on real held-out scenes.** Compare real-only against real+sim with the same model, target-conditioning and image-processing configuration. Match source scene groups, use repeated seeds for promising conditions, and measure grasp/placement/whole-scene success, intervention, cycle time and clipping. Simulation validation is a debugging screen; retain a physical hold-out set as the decision criterion.

For a proposed 32-condition matrix (2 models × 2 marker conditions × 4 augmentation conditions × 2 domain mixtures), all 32 runs can be specified, but stagger execution: establish input validity and a baseline first, then run matched pairs. Treat 80:20 as an initial conservative domain-mixture hypothesis. If sim helps, expand the sampling-ratio sweep; if it hurts, inspect contact/camera/action mismatch before generating thousands more examples. Synthetic pretraining followed by real-only fine-tuning is a separate later condition, not interchangeable with simultaneous co-training.
